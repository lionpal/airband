airband

How it works

airband has three parts. A small program on your computer listens to your coding agents. An app on your phone shows you what they said and takes your answer. A relay carries messages between the two when your phone is away from home, and you can turn it off.

The parts

PartWhere it runsWhat it does
airband (the airband command)Your computerHears from Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode. Writes the short version of each message with the model you chose. Keeps the chat. Hands your answers to the right agent.
The appYour phoneThe chat. Listening and speaking happen here: with a model on the phone, or with a service you gave a key for, called from the phone.
The relayrelay.airband.dev, or your ownPasses encrypted messages between phone and computer. Keeps them for your computer for up to seven days if it is off. Hands notifications to Apple. It cannot read any of it.

Two ways to connect

You choose when you pair. The first is the usual one. The second is for when nothing of yours should pass through anyone else's server.

With the relay

Works anywhere

  • At home, the phone connects directly to your computer.
  • Away, messages go through the relay, encrypted with a key only your phone and computer hold.
  • It switches between the two by itself.
  • You get notifications and the lock screen banner.
Without the relay

Nothing leaves your network

  • The phone only ever connects directly to your computer.
  • It works on the same Wi-Fi, or anywhere if both are on Tailscale.
  • Nothing passes through our servers.
  • No notifications: those need the relay to reach Apple.

Turn it on with airband relay off before you pair.

Both paths are encrypted with a key only your phone and computer hold. In both, the summary model and your coding agents talk to their own providers from your computer, and a listening or voice service you chose is called from your phone. None of that passes through airband's servers.

What keeps it private

  • Pairing. Your computer shows a code. The phone scans it. The code holds a secret that only those two devices ever have, and every message between them is encrypted with keys made from it, on your network as well as through the relay.
  • The relay. It knows a random channel name and can check that a device belongs to it. It never has the secret, so it cannot read a message. The privacy page lists exactly what it keeps.
  • Keys. An API key you enter is tested once, then stored encrypted on the device that uses it, and never shown again.
  • Voice. Listening and speaking are the phone's job. With the on-phone models, no audio leaves the phone.
  • Logs. Nothing is uploaded. A record of failed connections stays on the phone until you copy it yourself.

Where to plug something in

Most additions are one data file. The files live in catalog/ and are built into the app and the command. A newer signed copy is fetched from airband.dev, and a fetched copy cannot change a command that is run or where a key is sent.

To addYou write
A model for summaries, a voice, or a listening service that speaks a protocol airband already knowsOne file in catalog/providers/: its address, how the key is presented, how to test a key, what it costs
A model that runs on the phoneOne file in catalog/models/: its files, sizes and checksums, which engine runs it
A coding tool that already speaks the Agent Client ProtocolOne file in catalog/tools/: how to start it
A coding tool with its own way of reportingThat file, plus one module in crates/airband-cli/src/adapters/
Anything else that should report to youNo code: call airband report from a script

The code

FolderWhat is in it
crates/airband-coreThe message formats, pairing and encryption, and the catalogue reader. Shared by everything.
crates/airband-cliThe airband command: the program on your computer and the adapters for each coding tool.
crates/airband-radioEverything the phone does that is not drawing: the chat's rules, sending and retrying, listening and speaking. Written once in Rust.
apps/appleThe iPhone, iPad and CarPlay app. A thin layer of Swift over the radio crate.
crates/airband-relayThe relay. Run your own with airband relay <address>.

The code is on GitHub. The wire formats are written down in docs/protocols.md.
